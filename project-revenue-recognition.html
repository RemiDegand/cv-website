Problem & context

How it started

A new model had to spread revenue over time and match the accounting.

Context

OSLO, with about €10M revenue a year, bills in several ways: one-time orders, multi-period contracts and bundles. Two models already existed:

  • The first calculated revenue from the invoices and worked, but put the full amount of each invoice on its invoice date.
  • The second, a revenue recognition model, was rarely used because its figures differed from the accounting by several hundred k€.

The problem

No correct reporting or BI dashboard could show revenue allocated over time.

2

Part 2 of 3

What I built: a data model that spreads revenue over the activity

The model in 3 steps

SourceInvoice lines from the SQL databaseMyReport Data

I split the invoice details into 3 tables, one per billing type, because each type had its own rules, formats and data: for example, discounts had to be applied to some types, while others already included them. This kept the data clean. I then consolidated the 3 tables and linked them to a time table to spread the revenue over the months.

Impact & tools

Impact

  • €350k → €8k annual revenue discrepancies: 98 % fewer errors than the previous model.
  • A realistic monthly view of performance, that follows the company's actual activity.
  • Reliable analyses for performance monitoring and decisions, with financial and operational data side by side.

Revenue analysis now reflects the company's real economic activity.

Tools

  • MyReport Data (Data modeling)
